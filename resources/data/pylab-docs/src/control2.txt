@ bode, bodeplot, bodemag
The frequency response as gain and phase over frequency: the Bode diagram.

`bode` gives the gain (absolute, not in dB) and the phase in degrees at frequencies in rad/s, as 1-by-1-by-n arrays for one input and output; drawn when nothing is asked for, gain in dB above and phase below over a logarithmic frequency axis. Unless given, the frequencies reach two decades beyond the poles and zeros; `{wmin, wmax}` gives a range. Several models are drawn together, each with an optional line style. `bodeplot` draws; `bodemag` draws the gain alone.

# Syntax
bode(sys1, sys2, ..., w)
    Drawn.
[mag, phase, wout] = bode(sys, w)
    The gain and phase.
bode(sys, {wmin, wmax})
    Over a range.
bodeplot(sys)
    Drawn.
bodemag(sys1, 'r--', sys2, w)
    The gain alone.
# Examples
## Two lags and one
G = tf(10, conv([1 1], [0.1 1]));
[mag, ph] = bode(G, [0.1 1 10 100]);
[squeeze(mag), squeeze(ph)]
bode(G, tf(10, [1 1]))
legend('two lags', 'one lag')
# See also
margin nyquist freqresp

@ nyquist, nichols, sigma
The frequency response in the complex plane, as gain against phase, and as singular values.

`nyquist` draws the response in the complex plane for positive frequencies and its mirror image for negative ones (dashed), with the critical point -1: the closed loop is stable when the curve encircles -1 counterclockwise once for each unstable open-loop pole. `nichols` draws the gain in dB against the phase in degrees. `sigma` gives the singular values of the response at each frequency, the gains of a model with several inputs and outputs in its most and least sensitive directions.

# Syntax
nyquist(sys)
    Drawn.
[re, im, wout] = nyquist(sys, w)
    The real and imaginary parts.
nichols(sys)
    Drawn.
[mag, phase, wout] = nichols(sys, w)
    Gain and phase.
sigma(sys)
    Drawn.
[sv, wout] = sigma(sys, w)
    The singular values, a column for each frequency.
# Examples
## A loop with a gain margin of 2.5
L = tf(4, conv([1 1], [1 2 2]));
[re, im] = nyquist(L, 2);
[re, im]
subplot(1, 2, 1), nyquist(L)
subplot(1, 2, 2), nichols(L), grid on
## The gains of two inputs and two outputs
G = ss([-1 0; 0 -10], eye(2), [1 1; 0 1], 0);
sv = sigma(G, 0);
sv'
svd(dcgain(G))'
sigma(G), grid on

bode margin

@ margin, allmargin
How far a loop is from instability: its gain and phase margins.

The gain margin is how much the loop gain can grow before the closed loop is unstable: 1/|L| where the phase crosses -180 degrees (at Wcg). The phase margin is how much more phase lag it can take: 180 degrees plus the phase where the gain crosses 1 (at Wcp). `margin` gives the smallest of them, or draws the Bode diagram with them marked and named in the title; `allmargin` gives every crossing, the delay margin (the phase margin over its frequency, the delay the loop can take) and whether the closed loop is stable.

# Syntax
[Gm, Pm, Wcg, Wcp] = margin(sys)
    The margins, the gain margin absolute.
margin(sys)
    Drawn.
S = allmargin(sys)
    Every margin, as a structure.
# Examples
## A lag and a lightly damped pair
L = tf(4, conv([1 1], [1 2 2]));
[Gm, Pm, Wcg, Wcp] = margin(L);
[20*log10(Gm), Pm, Wcg, Wcp]
S = allmargin(L)
margin(L)

@ rlocus
The root locus: where the closed loop's poles go as the loop gain grows.

For a loop L in negative feedback with gain k, the closed-loop poles are the roots of den + k*num. They start at the open-loop poles (x) when k is 0 and end at the zeros (o), or go off to infinity along asymptotes, as k grows. `rlocus` draws the branches over gains spaced logarithmically, or gives the poles at the gains asked for (a column for each gain).

# Syntax
rlocus(sys)
    Drawn.
[r, k] = rlocus(sys)
    The poles and the gains.
r = rlocus(sys, k)
    At the gains given.
# Examples
## A loop with three poles and a zero
L = tf([1 3], [1 3 2 0]);
r = rlocus(L, [1 10])
roots([1 3 2 0] + 10*[0 0 1 3]).'
rlocus(L)

@ frd, frdata
A model given by its frequency response alone, as measured.

An `frd` model holds a response at a list of frequencies: ny-by-nu-by-n, or a vector for one input and output, the frequencies in rad/s unless `'FrequencyUnit'` is `'Hz'`. It adds, multiplies and divides with other models (each evaluated at its frequencies), closes loops with `feedback`, and is drawn by `bode` and `bodemag` at its own frequencies, the only ones it has. `frdata` gives the response and the frequencies back.

# Syntax
sys = frd(response, freq)
    From data.
sys = frd(sys, freq)
    A model's response.
[response, freq] = frdata(sys, 'v')
    Back as a vector.
# Examples
## A measured plant in a loop
w = logspace(-1, 2, 31);
H = squeeze(freqresp(tf(5, [1 2 5]), w));
F = frd(H, w);
[resp, f] = frdata(F, 'v');
size(resp)
C = feedback(F, 1);
[m, p] = bode(C, 1);
[m, p]
[abs(5/(9 + 2i)), angle(5/(9 + 2i))*180/pi]
bodemag(F, tf(5, [1 2 5]), 'r--', w)
legend('measured', 'model')

@ ctrb, obsv, ctrbf, obsvf
Whether every state can be steered by the inputs, and seen in the outputs.

`ctrb` gives the controllability matrix [B, AB, A^2 B, ...] and `obsv` the observability matrix [C; CA; CA^2; ...]: the model is controllable (observable) when its rank is the number of states. `ctrbf` and `obsvf` transform the model orthogonally so that the states that cannot be steered (seen) come first: `k` gives how many controllable (observable) states each step of the staircase found, `sum(k)` of them in all.

# Syntax
Co = ctrb(A, B)
    The controllability matrix.
Ob = obsv(A, C)
    The observability matrix.
[Abar, Bbar, Cbar, T, k] = ctrbf(A, B, C)
    The controllable states last.
[Abar, Bbar, Cbar, T, k] = obsvf(A, B, C)
    The observable states last.
# Examples
## A state the input never reaches
A = [1 1 0; 0 1 0; 0 0 2]; B = [0; 1; 0]; C = [1 0 1];
Co = ctrb(A, B)
rank(Co)
rank(obsv(A, C))
[Abar, Bbar, Cbar, T, k] = ctrbf(A, B, C);
k
round(Abar, 4)

@ lyap, dlyap, gram, covar
Lyapunov equations, and the Gramians and covariances they give.

`lyap` solves A X + X A' + Q = 0 (or the Sylvester equation A X + X B + C = 0), `dlyap` the discrete A X A' - X + Q = 0. For a stable model, `gram` gives the controllability Gramian (A Wc + Wc A' + B B' = 0), how strongly the inputs reach each state, or the observability Gramian. `covar` gives the steady output covariance of a model driven by white noise of intensity W, C Wc C' + D W D' with Wc the Gramian of B W B'.

# Syntax
X = lyap(A, Q)
    Continuous.
X = lyap(A, B, C)
    Sylvester.
X = dlyap(A, Q)
    Discrete.
Wc = gram(sys, 'c')
    Controllability; 'o' for observability.
[P, Q] = covar(sys, W)
    Output and state covariances.
# Examples
## A stable pair of states
A = [-1 2; 0 -3];
X = lyap(A, eye(2))
norm(A*X + X*A' + eye(2))
Ad = [0.5 0.2; 0 0.8];
Xd = dlyap(Ad, eye(2));
norm(Ad*Xd*Ad' - Xd + eye(2)) < 1e-12
sys = ss(A, [0; 1], [1 0], 0);
Wc = gram(sys, 'c')
P = covar(sys, 1)

@ care, dare
Algebraic Riccati equations, continuous and discrete.

`care` gives the stabilizing solution X of A'X + XA - (XB + S) R^-1 (B'X + S') + Q = 0, `dare` that of A'XA - X - (A'XB + S)(B'XB + R)^-1 (B'XA + S') + Q = 0, with R the identity and S zero unless given. L gives the closed-loop eigenvalues and G the gain: the solutions behind the linear-quadratic regulator and the Kalman filter.

# Syntax
X = care(A, B, Q)
    Continuous.
[X, L, G] = care(A, B, Q, R, S)
    With the eigenvalues and the gain.
[X, L, G] = dare(A, B, Q, R, S)
    Discrete.
# Examples
## A double integrator
A = [0 1; 0 0]; B = [0; 1];
[X, L, G] = care(A, B, eye(2))
Ad = [1 0.1; 0 1]; Bd = [0.005; 0.1];
[Xd, Ld] = dare(Ad, Bd, eye(2), 1);
abs(Ld)'
# See also
lqr lqe

@ lqr, dlqr, lqry, lqi
The linear-quadratic regulator: the state feedback that balances the states' size against the input's.

`lqr` gives the gain K of u = -Kx that makes the integral of x'Qx + u'Ru (+ 2x'Nu) smallest, with S the Riccati solution and P the closed-loop poles; of a discrete model, or with `dlqr`, the sum over samples. `lqry` weights the outputs, y'Qy, instead of the states. `lqi` adds integrators of the error r - y to the plant, so that the loop follows a constant reference with no error left: u = -K*[x; xi].

# Syntax
[K, S, P] = lqr(sys, Q, R)
    Of a model.
[K, S, P] = lqr(A, B, Q, R, N)
    Of matrices.
[K, S, e] = dlqr(A, B, Q, R)
    Discrete.
[K, S, e] = lqry(sys, Q, R)
    Weighting the outputs.
[K, S, e] = lqi(sys, Q, R)
    With integral action.
# Examples
## A double integrator, in time and sampled
A = [0 1; 0 0]; B = [0; 1];
[K, S, P] = lqr(A, B, eye(2), 1)
Kd = dlqr([1 0.1; 0 1], [0.005; 0.1], eye(2), 1)
## Outputs weighted, and an integrator added
sys = ss([0 1; -2 -3], [0; 1], [1 0], 0);
Ky = lqry(sys, 10, 1)
lqr(sys, sys.C'*10*sys.C, 1)
Ki = lqi(sys, diag([1 1 10]), 1)

care place kalman reg

@ lqe, kalman, estim, reg
State estimators: the Kalman filter, and a regulator built from an estimator and a state feedback.

For x' = Ax + Bu + Gw, y = Cx + Du + v, with process noise w and measurement noise v of covariances Q and R, `lqe` gives the gain L of the estimator xhat' = A xhat + Bu + L(y - C xhat - Du) that makes the error's covariance P smallest. `kalman` builds that estimator from a model whose last inputs are the noise w: it takes [u; y] and gives [yhat; xhat]. `estim` builds an estimator from any gain; `reg` joins one to a state feedback K into a controller from y to u = -K xhat. By the separation principle the closed loop's poles are those of A - BK and of A - LC.

# Syntax
[L, P, E] = lqe(A, G, C, Q, R)
    The estimator gain.
[kest, L, P] = kalman(sys, Qn, Rn)
    The estimator as a model.
est = estim(sys, L)
    From a gain.
rsys = reg(sys, K, L)
    The controller.
# Examples
## An oscillator measured with noise
A = [0 1; -1 -0.2]; B = [0; 1]; C = [1 0];
[L, P, E] = lqe(A, B, C, 1, 0.1);
L
[kest, L2] = kalman(ss(A, [B B], C, 0), 1, 0.1);
size(kest)
norm(L2 - L) < 1e-10
K = lqr(A, B, eye(2), 1);
R = reg(ss(A, B, C, 0), K, L);
CL = feedback(ss(A, B, C, 0), R, 1);
sort(abs(pole(CL)))'
sort(abs([eig(A - B*K); eig(A - L*C)]))'

@ place, acker
Pole placement: the state feedback that puts the closed loop's poles where they are asked for.

Both give K such that the poles of A - BK are p. `place` uses a robust method that also takes several inputs and keeps the gain well conditioned (no pole may be asked for more times than there are inputs); `acker` uses Ackermann's formula, for a single input, simple but ill-conditioned for many states.

# Syntax
K = place(A, B, p)
    Robust placement.
k = acker(A, b, p)
    Ackermann's formula.
# Examples
## Poles at -1, -2, -3 moved to -2, -3, -4
A = [0 1 0; 0 0 1; -6 -11 -6]; B = [0; 0; 1];
p = [-2 -3 -4];
K = place(A, B, p)
k = acker(A, B, p)
sort(eig(A - B*K))'

@ getPeakGain, hinfnorm
The largest gain of a model over all frequencies: its H-infinity norm.

`getPeakGain` gives the largest singular value of the frequency response and the frequency where it is, found on a fine grid about the poles and zeros and each peak refined. `hinfnorm` gives the same for a stable model, and Inf for an unstable one, whose response to a bounded input grows without bound.

# Syntax
[gpeak, fpeak] = getPeakGain(sys)
    The peak gain and its frequency.
[ninf, fpeak] = hinfnorm(sys)
    The H-infinity norm.
# Examples
## A resonance against its formula
G = tf(1, [1 0.2 1]);
[g, f] = getPeakGain(G)
zeta = 0.1;
[1/(2*zeta*sqrt(1 - zeta^2)), sqrt(1 - 2*zeta^2)]
hinfnorm(tf(1, [1 -1]))

@ hsvd, balreal, balred, modred
Model reduction: the states that matter least to the input-output behaviour, left out.

The Hankel singular values (`hsvd`) measure how much each state of a balanced realization takes part in the response: `balreal` gives that realization, in which the controllability and observability Gramians are both diag(g). `balred` keeps the r states of the largest values and leaves out the rest, by default with their derivatives set to zero so that the gain at rest is kept (`'Truncate'` drops them). `modred` leaves out the states named, of any realization.

# Syntax
hsv = hsvd(sys)
    The Hankel singular values.
[sysb, g] = balreal(sys)
    Balanced.
rsys = balred(sys, r)
    r states, the gain at rest kept.
rsys = balred(sys, r, 'Truncate')
    Truncated.
rsys = modred(sys, elim, 'Truncate')
    The states elim left out.
# Examples
## Four poles reduced to two
G = ss(tf(400, conv(conv([1 1], [1 2]), conv([1 10], [1 20]))));
hsv = hsvd(G)'
[Gb, g] = balreal(G);
diag(gram(Gb, 'o'))'
G2 = balred(G, 2);
G2t = balred(G, 2, 'Truncate');
[dcgain(G), dcgain(G2), dcgain(G2t)]
dcgain(modred(Gb, 3:4, 'Truncate'))
bodemag(G, G2, 'r--', G2t, 'g:')
legend('four states', 'two, gain at rest kept', 'two, truncated')
