@ contour3, contourc, clabel
Contour lines drawn at their heights, the contour matrix without drawing, and labels on the lines.

`contour3` draws the contour lines of a matrix at their own heights in 3-D. `contourc` computes them without drawing: a matrix of segments, each a column [level; number of points] followed by the points' columns [x; y]. `clabel` writes each line's value on it, given the contour matrix and the contour object that `contour` returns.

# Syntax
contour3(X, Y, Z, n)
    n levels at their heights.
C = contourc(x, y, Z, levels)
    The lines as numbers.
clabel(C, h)
    Values on the lines.
# Examples
## A hill's contours, flat and in 3-D
[X, Y] = meshgrid(-2:0.1:2);
Z = exp(-X.^2 - Y.^2);
C = contourc(-2:0.1:2, -2:0.1:2, Z, [0.5 0.5]);
C(:, 1)'
n = C(2, 1);
r = hypot(C(1, 2:n+1), C(2, 2:n+1));
[min(r), max(r)]
sqrt(log(2))
subplot(1, 2, 1), [C2, h] = contour(X, Y, Z, 0.2:0.2:0.8); clabel(C2, h), axis equal
subplot(1, 2, 2), contour3(X, Y, Z, 10), view(-30, 30)
# See also
contour contourf

@ fcontour, ezcontour, ezcontourf
Contours of a function of two variables, drawn over a range.

`fcontour` evaluates a function handle f(x, y) (written for arrays) over [-5 5] in each direction unless given and draws its contours. `ezcontour` and `ezcontourf` are older forms that also take text such as `'x*exp(-x^2 - y^2)'`, over [-2*pi, 2*pi] unless given, titled with the function; `ezcontourf` fills between the lines.

# Syntax
fcontour(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax])
    Contours of f.
ezcontour(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax], n)
    The older form, n points a side.
ezcontourf(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax], n)
    Filled.
# Examples
## One function three ways
f = @(x, y) x.*exp(-x.^2 - y.^2);
subplot(1, 3, 1), fcontour(f, [-2 2 -2 2]), axis equal
subplot(1, 3, 2), ezcontour('x*exp(-x^2 - y^2)', [-2 2 -2 2], 40), axis equal
subplot(1, 3, 3), ezcontourf('x*exp(-x^2 - y^2)', [-2 2 -2 2], 40), axis equal
# See also
contour fsurf fimplicit

@ surfc, meshc, meshz, waterfall, surfl
Surfaces and meshes with more: contours under them, a curtain round the edges, a row of curtains, and lighting.

`surfc` and `meshc` draw a surface or a mesh with its contour lines on the floor below. `meshz` hangs a curtain from the mesh's edges down to the lowest value; `waterfall` draws each row of the matrix as a curtain of its own, like a row of profiles. `surfl` draws a surface lit from a light behind the viewer, coloured by how much light each face catches.

# Syntax
surfc(X, Y, Z)
    With contours below.
meshc(X, Y, Z)
    A mesh with contours.
meshz(X, Y, Z)
    With a curtain.
waterfall(X, Y, Z)
    Rows as curtains.
surfl(X, Y, Z)
    Lit.
# Examples
## One surface five ways
[X, Y, Z] = peaks(25);
subplot(2, 3, 1), surfc(X, Y, Z), title('surfc')
subplot(2, 3, 2), meshc(X, Y, Z), title('meshc')
subplot(2, 3, 3), meshz(X, Y, Z), title('meshz')
subplot(2, 3, 4), waterfall(X, Y, Z), title('waterfall')
subplot(2, 3, 5), surfl(X, Y, Z), shading interp, title('surfl')
# See also
surf mesh contour

@ pcolor, surface
A pseudocolour plot, and a surface added to the axes without clearing them.

`pcolor` draws a matrix as a flat surface seen from above, each cell coloured by the value at its corner (so the last row and column set no cell's colour unless shading is interpolated). `surface` adds a surface object to the current axes as they are, without clearing them or changing the view, and takes its properties by name.

# Syntax
pcolor(X, Y, C)
    Cells coloured by value.
h = surface(X, Y, Z, 'Name', value)
    A surface added.
# Examples
## A field on a curved grid, and a surface added
[R, T] = meshgrid(linspace(1, 2, 12), linspace(0, pi, 24));
X = R.*cos(T); Y = R.*sin(T);
subplot(1, 2, 1), pcolor(X, Y, R.*sin(3*T)), axis equal
subplot(1, 2, 2), plot3([0 0], [0 0], [-1 1], 'k', 'LineWidth', 2), hold on
[x, y] = meshgrid(-1:0.25:1);
surface(x, y, 0.5*x.*y, 'FaceColor', 'interp'), view(3), hold off

surf image

@ fmesh, ezsurf, ezmesh, ezsurfc, ezmeshc
Surfaces and meshes of a function of two variables.

`fmesh` evaluates a function handle f(x, y) over [-5 5] in each direction unless given and draws a mesh. The older `ezsurf`, `ezmesh`, `ezsurfc` and `ezmeshc` also take text such as `'sin(x)*cos(y)'` (its variables in alphabetical order), over [-2*pi, 2*pi] on a 60-by-60 grid unless given, titled with the function; the c forms add contours below.

# Syntax
fmesh(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax])
    A mesh of f.
ezsurf(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax], n)
    A surface.
ezmesh(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax], n)
    A mesh.
ezsurfc(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax], n)
    A surface with contours.
ezmeshc(f, [xmin xmax ymin ymax], n)
    A mesh with contours.
# Examples
## A ripple
subplot(1, 2, 1), fmesh(@(x, y) sin(x).*cos(y), [-3 3 -3 3])
subplot(1, 2, 2), ezsurfc('sin(x)*cos(y)', [-3 3 -3 3], 30)
# See also
fsurf fcontour

@ peaks, sphere, cylinder, ellipsoid
Surfaces to try things with: a function with peaks and valleys, a sphere, a cylinder of any profile, an ellipsoid.

`peaks` gives a smooth function of two variables with three peaks and two valleys on [-3, 3] (49 points a side unless given). `sphere`, `cylinder` and `ellipsoid` give the coordinates of their surfaces for `surf` or `mesh`, or draw them when no output is asked for; `cylinder` takes a radius profile from bottom to top, so a vector of radii makes a surface of revolution.

# Syntax
[X, Y, Z] = peaks(n)
    The peaks function.
[X, Y, Z] = sphere(n)
    A unit sphere.
[X, Y, Z] = cylinder(r, n)
    A surface of revolution.
[X, Y, Z] = ellipsoid(xc, yc, zc, xr, yr, zr, n)
    An ellipsoid.
# Examples
## Four shapes
[X, Y, Z] = peaks(30);
[min(Z(:)), max(Z(:))]
subplot(2, 2, 1), surf(X, Y, Z)
[x, y, z] = sphere(20);
subplot(2, 2, 2), surf(x, y, z), axis equal
[x, y, z] = cylinder(1 + 0.3*sin(linspace(0, 2*pi, 15)), 30);
subplot(2, 2, 3), surf(x, y, z), axis equal
[x, y, z] = ellipsoid(0, 0, 0, 3, 2, 1, 20);
subplot(2, 2, 4), surf(x, y, z), axis equal
# See also
surf meshgrid

@ shading, lighting, light, camlight, material
How surfaces look: flat or smooth colour, lights, and how lit faces are shaded.

`shading` sets how surfaces in the axes are coloured: `faceted` (flat with black edges, the default), `flat` (no edges) or `interp` (colours blended across each face). `light` adds a light at a position and `camlight` one near the camera; `lighting` then shades lit faces `flat` or `gouraud` (smooth), or turns lighting off with `none`. `material` sets how the surfaces and patches reflect light (`shiny`, `dull`, `metal`, or the strengths as numbers): their reflectance properties, which the page keeps but does not draw.

# Syntax
shading interp
    Smooth colour.
light('Position', [x y z])
    A light.
camlight headlight
    A light at the camera.
lighting gouraud
    Smooth lighting.
material shiny
    The reflectances of a material.
# Examples
## One sphere three ways
[x, y, z] = sphere(30);
subplot(1, 3, 1), surf(x, y, z), axis equal, title('faceted')
subplot(1, 3, 2), surf(x, y, z), shading interp, axis equal, title('interp')
subplot(1, 3, 3), surf(x, y, z, 'FaceColor', [0.8 0.5 0.2], 'EdgeColor', 'none'), axis equal
camlight headlight, lighting gouraud, title('lit')
# See also
surf colormap

@ clim, caxis, alpha, hidden
The limits of the colour scale, and two older settings that are accepted.

`clim` sets the values that map to the first and last colours of the colormap (values beyond take the end colours), or gives them; `clim auto` lets the data set them again. `caxis` is its older name. `alpha` and `hidden` are accepted and have no effect: transparency is set by an object's FaceAlpha, and meshes are drawn with hidden lines removed.

# Syntax
clim([cmin cmax])
    Set the limits.
lims = clim
    The limits.
clim auto
    From the data.
caxis([cmin cmax])
    The older name.
alpha(value)
    Accepted.
hidden off
    Accepted.
# Examples
## The same data on two scales
Z = peaks(30);
subplot(1, 2, 1), imagesc(Z), colorbar, axis image
clim
subplot(1, 2, 2), imagesc(Z), colorbar, axis image
clim([-2 2])
# See also
colormap colorbar imagesc

@ camzoom, camva, camlookat
The camera: zoom by a factor, its view angle, and pointing it at objects.

The axes are drawn as a camera sees them, from a position toward a target, with a view angle: the smaller the angle, the larger the axes. `camzoom` narrows the angle by a factor (above 1 nearer, below 1 farther); `camva` sets or gives the angle itself. `camlookat` points the camera at objects and closes in until they fill the view.

# Syntax
camzoom(f)
    Zoom by a factor.
va = camva
    The view angle.
camva(va)
    Set it.
camlookat(h)
    Look at objects.
# Examples
## Half as near again
[x, y, z] = sphere(16);
surf(x, y, z), axis equal
va = camva
camzoom(1.5)
camva
## At the last of three spheres
[x, y, z] = sphere(16);
hold on
for k = 0:2
    h(k + 1) = surf(x + 3*k, y, z);
end
hold off, axis equal, view(3)
camlookat(h(3))
camva

view campos
