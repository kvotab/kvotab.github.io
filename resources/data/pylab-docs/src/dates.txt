@ tic, toc, cputime
A stopwatch, and the processor time used.

`tic` starts the stopwatch and `toc` gives the seconds since. A stopwatch of one's own (`t0 = tic`, then `toc(t0)`) lets several run at once without disturbing each other. `cputime` gives the processor time used so far, which differs from the time on the clock when the program waits, or works on several cores.

# Syntax
tic
    The stopwatch started.
t0 = tic
    A stopwatch of one's own.
toc
    The seconds since tic, printed.
t = toc
    The seconds since tic.
t = toc(t0)
    The seconds on a stopwatch of one's own.
t = cputime
    The processor time used, in seconds.
# Examples
## A computation timed
> The times differ from run to run, so the example shows what they are, not their values.
t0 = tic;
c0 = cputime;
A = rand(400);
B = A*A';
elapsed = toc(t0);
used = cputime - c0;
class(t0)
[elapsed > 0, used >= 0]

pause clock profile timeit

@ pause
Waiting: for a number of seconds, or for a key.

`pause(s)` waits s seconds (fractions too) and lets the page draw what it has to; `pause` with no input waits until a key is pressed in the Command Window. For a player or recorder of sound, `pause(player)` stops it where it is, and `resume` goes on.

# Syntax
pause(seconds)
    Waiting so long.
pause
    Waiting for a key.
pause(player)
    A player or recorder stopped where it is.
# Examples
## A quarter of a second
t0 = tic;
pause(0.25)
toc(t0) >= 0.25
## An animation drawn step by step
> Each pause gives the page time to draw the new frame.
x = linspace(0, 2*pi, 100);
h = plot(x, sin(x));
for k = 1:20
    h.YData = sin(x + k/5);
    pause(0.01)
end

@ clock, now, date
The date and time now: as a vector, a serial date number, and text.

`clock` gives [year month day hour minute seconds] (the seconds with their fraction), `now` the serial date number (days from the year 0, the time as a fraction) and `date` today's date as 'dd-mmm-yyyy'. All three are the computer's local time. `datetime('now')` gives the same moment as a datetime.

# Syntax
c = clock
    Year, month, day, hour, minute, seconds.
t = now
    A serial date number.
s = date
    Today's date as text.
# Examples
## The same moment three ways
> The values change with the time the example is run; the checks show how they agree.
c = clock;
t = now;
s = date;
numel(c)
abs(datenum(c) - t) < 1/24/3600
strcmp(s, datestr(floor(t), 'dd-mmm-yyyy'))
# See also
datetime datenum datestr

@ datenum, datevec, datestr
Dates and times as serial numbers, as vectors, and as text.

A serial date number counts days from the year 0 (1 January of year 0 is day 1), with the time of day as a fraction: differences are days, and adding 1 is the next day. `datenum` makes them from years, months, days (and hours, minutes, seconds; months and days past their ends roll over), from date vectors or from text; `datevec` gives [year month day hour minute seconds]; `datestr` gives text, in a numbered format (0 to 31) or a pattern of `yyyy`, `yy`, `mmmm`, `mmm`, `mm`, `dd`, `ddd`, `HH`, `MM`, `SS`, `FFF` and `PM`. Datetimes do the same work with time zones and formats of their own.

# Syntax
n = datenum(Y, M, D)
    A date.
n = datenum(Y, M, D, h, m, s)
    A date and time.
n = datenum(datevector)
    From [year month day hour minute seconds].
n = datenum(text, format)
    From text.
v = datevec(t)
    The date vector.
[Y, M, D, h, m, s] = datevec(t)
    Its parts.
s = datestr(t)
    Text.
s = datestr(t, format)
    Text in a format.
# Examples
## Days between two dates, and a time as a fraction of a day
a = datenum(2026, 11, 5);
b = datenum(2027, 1, 1);
b - a
t = datenum(2026, 11, 5, 18, 0, 0);
t - a
datestr(t)
datestr(t, 'yyyy-mm-dd HH:MM')
datestr(t, 29)
## Text to vectors, and months past their end
v = datevec('2026-11-05 14:30', 'yyyy-mm-dd HH:MM')
datestr(datenum(2026, 14, 1))
datestr(datenum(2026, 3, 0))
# See also
datetime weekday clock

@ weekday, eomday, leapyear, calendar
Days of the week, the ends of months, leap years and a month's calendar.

`weekday` gives 1 for Sunday to 7 for Saturday, and the day's short name; `eomday` the last day of a month (29 for February in a leap year); `leapyear` whether a year is one (divisible by 4, except centuries not divisible by 400). `calendar` gives a month as a 6-by-7 matrix, weeks from Sunday to Saturday with 0 outside the month, and prints it when there is no output.

# Syntax
[n, name] = weekday(t)
    The day of the week, and its name.
d = eomday(year, month)
    The last day of a month.
tf = leapyear(year)
    True for leap years.
c = calendar(y, m)
    A month's days in a matrix.
calendar(y, m)
    The month printed.
# Examples
## A week's days, February's lengths
[n, name] = weekday(datenum(2026, 11, 5:7))
eomday([2024 2025 2026], 2)
leapyear([1900 2000 2024 2026])
## A month printed
calendar(2026, 11)
# See also
datenum day isweekend

@ addtodate, etime
Serial date numbers moved by calendar amounts; the seconds between two date vectors.

`addtodate` adds whole years, months, weeks or days, or hours, minutes, seconds or milliseconds, to serial date numbers (a month later is the same day of the next month). `etime` gives the seconds from one date vector (as `clock` gives) to another.

# Syntax
t = addtodate(t0, q, field)
    Moved by q of field.
e = etime(t1, t0)
    Seconds from t0 to t1.
# Examples
## Next month and the coming weeks
t0 = datenum(2026, 11, 5);
datestr(addtodate(t0, 1, 'month'))
datestr(addtodate(t0, 1:3, 'week'))
datestr(addtodate(t0, 90, 'minute'))
## Seconds between two times
etime([2026 11 5 14 30 0], [2026 11 5 12 0 0])
