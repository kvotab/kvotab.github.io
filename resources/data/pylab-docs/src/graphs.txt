@ graph, digraph
Graphs: nodes joined by edges, undirected or directed, the edges with weights or without.

A graph is made from end nodes s and t (numbers or names, an edge from each s(k) to t(k)), with weights, or from an adjacency matrix (A(i, j) nonzero an edge, its value the weight). `graph` makes an undirected one (an edge joins two nodes both ways), `digraph` a directed one (an edge goes from s to t). G.Edges is a table of EndNodes and Weight, kept sorted; G.Nodes a table of the nodes' Name and anything else added. `plot` draws a graph, a digraph's edges with arrowheads.

# Syntax
G = graph(s, t, weights)
    From end nodes.
G = graph(A, nodenames)
    From an adjacency matrix.
G = digraph(s, t, weights)
    Directed.
G = digraph(EdgeTable, NodeTable)
    From tables.
# Examples
## Five towns and their roads, and a chain of tasks
G = graph([1 1 2 3 3 4], [2 3 3 4 5 5], [4 2 1 5 8 3]);
G.Edges
D = digraph({'plan', 'plan', 'build', 'test'}, {'build', 'test', 'ship', 'ship'});
D.Nodes
subplot(1, 2, 1), plot(G, 'EdgeLabel', G.Edges.Weight)
subplot(1, 2, 2), plot(D)

@ numnodes, numedges, findnode, findedge, edgecount, ismultigraph
A graph's size, and its nodes and edges found by name or by their ends.

`numnodes` and `numedges` count; `findnode` gives the numbers of nodes named (0 for a name not there); `findedge` the number of the edge joining two nodes (0 where none does), or the end nodes of edges given by number. `edgecount` counts the edges between two nodes, and `ismultigraph` asks whether any pair is joined more than once.

# Syntax
n = numnodes(G)
    Nodes.
m = numedges(G)
    Edges.
ids = findnode(G, names)
    Nodes by name.
idx = findedge(G, s, t)
    Edges by their ends.
[s, t] = findedge(G, idx)
    Ends by edge.
c = edgecount(G, s, t)
    How many join s and t.
tf = ismultigraph(G)
    Any pair joined twice.
# Examples
## Finding things
D = digraph({'a', 'b', 'b', 'c'}, {'b', 'c', 'd', 'd'});
[numnodes(D), numedges(D)]
findnode(D, {'c', 'x'})
findedge(D, 'b', 'd')
[s, t] = findedge(D, 3)
G = graph([1 1 2], [2 3 3]);
edgecount(G, 2, 1)
ismultigraph(addedge(G, 1, 2))

@ degree, indegree, outdegree, neighbors, successors, predecessors, outedges, inedges
The edges at each node, and the nodes and edges next to a node.

`degree` counts the edges meeting each node (a self-loop twice; for a digraph, in and out together), `indegree` and `outdegree` those coming into and leaving a node of a digraph. `neighbors` gives the nodes joined to a node either way, `successors` those a digraph's node points to and `predecessors` those pointing to it. `outedges` and `inedges` give the edges themselves, with the nodes at their other ends.

# Syntax
d = degree(G, nodes)
    Edges at each node.
d = indegree(G)
    Coming in.
d = outdegree(G)
    Going out.
N = neighbors(G, node)
    Joined either way.
[s, eid] = successors(G, node)
    Pointed to, and the edges.
p = predecessors(G, node)
    Pointing here.
[eid, nid] = outedges(G, node)
    The edges out, and their ends.
eid = inedges(G, node)
    The edges in.
# Examples
## A small directed network
D = digraph([1 1 2 3 4 4], [2 3 3 4 1 2]);
[indegree(D), outdegree(D), degree(D)]
successors(D, 4)'
predecessors(D, 3)'
neighbors(D, 1)'
[eid, nid] = outedges(D, 4)

graph adjacency

@ adjacency, incidence
A graph as a sparse matrix: who is joined to whom, and which edge meets which node.

`adjacency` gives A(i, j) = 1 where an edge joins i to j (both ways for a graph), or the weights there with `'weighted'`. `incidence` gives a row for each node and a column for each edge: -1 at an edge's source, 1 at its target. For a graph, I*I' is the Laplacian, the degrees on the diagonal.

# Syntax
A = adjacency(G)
    Ones where joined.
A = adjacency(G, 'weighted')
    The weights.
I = incidence(G)
    Nodes by edges.
# Examples
## A weighted triangle with a tail
G = graph([1 1 2 3], [2 3 3 4], [5 2 7 1]);
full(adjacency(G))
full(adjacency(G, 'weighted'))
I = full(incidence(G))
isequal(I*I', full(laplacian(G)))

@ addedge, rmedge, addnode, rmnode, subgraph, reordernodes, flipedge
New graphs from a graph: edges and nodes added or taken away, a part, renumbered, or turned round.

A graph is a value: each of these gives a new graph and leaves the one given as it was. `addedge` adds edges (and nodes named or numbered past the last), `rmedge` takes edges away, `addnode` adds nodes joined to nothing, `rmnode` takes nodes away with their edges (the nodes after them numbered down). `subgraph` keeps some nodes and the edges among them, `reordernodes` numbers the nodes in another order, and `flipedge` turns a digraph's edges round.

# Syntax
H = addedge(G, s, t, weights)
    Edges added.
H = rmedge(G, s, t)
    Edges taken away.
H = addnode(G, names)
    Nodes added.
H = rmnode(G, nodes)
    Nodes taken away.
H = subgraph(G, nodes)
    A part.
H = reordernodes(G, order)
    Renumbered.
H = flipedge(G, s, t)
    Edges turned round.
# Examples
## Editing a graph
G = graph({'a', 'b', 'c'}, {'b', 'c', 'a'});
G = addedge(G, 'c', 'd');
G = addnode(G, 'e');
G.Nodes.Name'
H = rmnode(G, 'a');
H.Edges
S = subgraph(G, {'a', 'b', 'c'});
numedges(S)
R = reordernodes(G, [5 4 3 2 1]);
R.Nodes.Name'
D = flipedge(digraph([1 2], [2 3]));
D.Edges.EndNodes

@ shortestpath, distances, nearest, shortestpathtree
Shortest paths: between two nodes, between every pair, the nodes within a distance, and the tree of paths from a node.

A path's length is the sum of its edges' weights (the number of its edges when there are none, or with `'Method', 'unweighted'`). `shortestpath` gives the nodes along one shortest path, its length and its edges; `distances` the lengths between every pair (Inf where there is no path); `nearest` the nodes within a distance of a node, nearest first; `shortestpathtree` the digraph of the shortest paths from a node to every other. Negative weights need a digraph without a negative cycle (`'Method', 'mixed'`).

# Syntax
[P, d, edgepath] = shortestpath(G, s, t)
    One shortest path.
d = distances(G)
    Every pair.
[nodeIDs, dist] = nearest(G, s, d)
    Within a distance.
TR = shortestpathtree(G, s)
    The tree from s.
# Examples
## Roads between five towns
G = graph([1 1 2 3 3 4], [2 3 3 4 5 5], [4 2 1 5 8 3]);
[P, d] = shortestpath(G, 1, 5)
distances(G)
[ids, dist] = nearest(G, 1, 6)
TR = shortestpathtree(G, 1);
h = plot(G, 'EdgeLabel', G.Edges.Weight);
highlight(h, TR, 'EdgeColor', 'r', 'LineWidth', 2)

@ bfsearch, dfsearch, allpaths
Walking a graph: breadth first, depth first, and every path between two nodes.

`bfsearch` visits the nodes in order of their distance in edges from the start, `dfsearch` goes as deep as it can along each branch before coming back; neighbours are taken in the order of their numbers, and `'Restart', true` goes on from the first node not reached until every node has been visited. `allpaths` lists every path between two nodes that visits no node twice, as node lists (and edge lists).

# Syntax
v = bfsearch(G, s)
    Breadth first.
v = dfsearch(G, s, 'Restart', true)
    Depth first, every node.
[paths, edgepaths] = allpaths(G, s, t)
    Every simple path.
# Examples
## A small tree with a shortcut
G = graph([1 1 2 2 3 4], [2 3 4 5 6 6]);
bfsearch(G, 1)'
dfsearch(G, 1)'
paths = allpaths(G, 1, 6);
paths{:}

@ conncomp, biconncomp, condensation
The parts of a graph: connected components, the parts no single node splits, and a digraph's components as nodes.

`conncomp` gives each node's component, components numbered in the order of their first nodes (a digraph's are strongly connected, each node reaching each other, unless `'Type', 'weak'`). `biconncomp` gives each edge's biconnected component and the cut vertices, whose removal splits the graph. `condensation` makes a digraph of the strongly connected components, which has no cycle.

# Syntax
[bins, binsizes] = conncomp(G)
    Each node's component.
bins = conncomp(G, 'Type', 'weak')
    Directions ignored.
[bins, iC] = biconncomp(G)
    Each edge's, and the cut vertices.
C = condensation(G)
    Components as nodes.
# Examples
## Two islands, and a bridge
G = graph([1 2 3 4 5 6], [2 3 1 5 6 4]);
[bins, sizes] = conncomp(G)
H = addedge(G, 3, 4);
[ebins, cut] = biconncomp(H)
D = digraph([1 2 3 3 4 5], [2 3 1 4 5 4]);
conncomp(D)
C = condensation(D);
C.Edges.EndNodes

@ toposort, isdag, hascycles, transclosure, transreduction
Order and reach in a digraph: an order every edge goes forward in, cycles, and the edges of reach.

`toposort` puts the nodes of a digraph without cycles in an order in which every edge goes forward (of the nodes free to come next, the smallest), and `isdag` asks whether there is no cycle; `hascycles` asks the same of any graph. `transclosure` adds an edge from each node to every node it reaches; `transreduction` keeps the fewest edges that reach as the digraph does.

# Syntax
order = toposort(G)
    Every edge forward.
tf = isdag(G)
    No cycle.
tf = hascycles(G)
    A cycle somewhere.
H = transclosure(G)
    Reach as edges.
H = transreduction(G)
    The fewest edges.
# Examples
## Tasks and what they wait for
D = digraph([1 1 2 3 4], [2 3 4 4 5]);
toposort(D)
[isdag(D), hascycles(D)]
T = transclosure(D);
numedges(T)
R = transreduction(addedge(D, 1, 4));
numedges(R)
isdag(addedge(D, 5, 1))

@ minspantree, maxflow
A minimum spanning tree, and the largest flow through a network.

`minspantree` joins the nodes a root reaches with edges of least total weight (`'Type', 'forest'` makes a tree in each component); pred gives each node's parent. `maxflow` treats weights as capacities and gives the largest flow from s to t, the flow on each edge as a digraph, and a minimum cut: the nodes on s's side and on t's, whose edges between carry exactly the maximum flow.

# Syntax
[T, pred] = minspantree(G)
    The tree, and parents.
T = minspantree(G, 'Type', 'forest')
    A tree per component.
[mf, GF, cs, ct] = maxflow(G, s, t)
    The flow and a cut.
# Examples
## Cables and pipes
G = graph([1 1 2 2 3 4], [2 3 3 4 4 5], [3 1 1 4 2 5]);
[T, pred] = minspantree(G);
T.Edges
sum(T.Edges.Weight)
D = digraph([1 1 2 2 3 4], [2 3 3 4 4 5], [3 2 1 2 3 4]);
[mf, GF, cs, ct] = maxflow(D, 1, 5)
subplot(1, 2, 1), h = plot(G, 'EdgeLabel', G.Edges.Weight); highlight(h, T, 'EdgeColor', 'r', 'LineWidth', 2)
subplot(1, 2, 2), plot(GF, 'EdgeLabel', GF.Edges.Weight)

@ centrality, isisomorphic
How central each node is, and whether two graphs are the same but for numbering.

`centrality` scores nodes: `'degree'` by their edges, `'closeness'` by how near they are to all others, `'betweenness'` by how many shortest paths pass through them, `'pagerank'` and `'eigenvector'` by the importance of their neighbours (these sum to 1); a digraph also has in and out kinds and `'hubs'` and `'authorities'`. `isisomorphic` asks whether the nodes of one graph can be renumbered to give the other.

# Syntax
C = centrality(G, type)
    A score per node.
C = centrality(G, type, 'Cost', c)
    Edge lengths for closeness and betweenness.
tf = isisomorphic(G1, G2)
    The same shape.
# Examples
## A star and a chain
G = graph([1 1 1 1 5], [2 3 4 5 6]);
centrality(G, 'degree')'
centrality(G, 'betweenness')'
round(centrality(G, 'closeness')', 4)
round(centrality(G, 'pagerank')', 4)
isisomorphic(G, graph([6 6 6 6 2], [1 3 4 2 5]))
isisomorphic(G, graph([1 2 3 4 5], [2 3 4 5 6]))

@ highlight, labelnode, labeledge, layout
A graph plot changed: nodes and edges drawn apart, labels, and the nodes placed again.

`highlight` draws nodes, edges or a whole subgraph (a path tree, a spanning tree) of a plot apart, in red and larger unless told otherwise; nodes given with `'EdgeColor'` or `'LineWidth'` mark the edges between them too, so a path draws as a path. `labelnode` and `labeledge` label nodes and edges. `layout` places the nodes again: `'force'`, `'layered'` (for a digraph without cycles), `'circle'` or `'subspace'`.

# Syntax
highlight(h, nodes, 'NodeColor', c)
    Nodes drawn apart.
highlight(h, s, t, 'EdgeColor', c, 'LineWidth', w)
    Edges drawn apart.
labelnode(h, nodes, labels)
    Node labels.
labeledge(h, s, t, labels)
    Edge labels.
layout(h, method)
    Placed again.
# Examples
## A path through a grid of streets
G = graph([1 2 1 3 2 4 3 5 4 6 5 6], [2 3 4 4 5 5 6 6 7 7 8 8]);
h = plot(G);
layout(h, 'circle')
P = shortestpath(G, 1, 8);
highlight(h, P, 'EdgeColor', 'r', 'LineWidth', 2)
labelnode(h, [1 8], {'start', 'end'})
labeledge(h, 1, 2, 'first')
