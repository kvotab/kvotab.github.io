@ wfilters, dbwavf, symwavf, coifwavf
The filters of a discrete wavelet: lowpass and highpass, for decomposition and reconstruction.

`wfilters` gives the four filters of a wavelet by name: `'haar'`, `'db1'` to `'db45'` (Daubechies), `'sym2'` to `'sym20'` (symlets, nearly symmetric), `'coif1'` to `'coif5'` (coiflets, with vanishing moments of the scaling function too), and the biorthogonal `'bior'` and `'rbio'` families. For an orthogonal wavelet the reconstruction lowpass filter LoR sums to sqrt(2) and has norm 1, LoD is LoR reversed, and the highpass filters alternate their signs. `dbwavf`, `symwavf` and `coifwavf` give the scaling filter LoR/sqrt(2), which sums to 1.

# Syntax
[LoD, HiD, LoR, HiR] = wfilters(wname)
    The four filters.
[F1, F2] = wfilters(wname, type)
    'd' decomposition, 'r' reconstruction, 'l' lowpass, 'h' highpass.
F = dbwavf(wname)
    A Daubechies scaling filter.
F = symwavf(wname)
    A symlet's.
F = coifwavf(wname)
    A coiflet's.
# Examples
## The four filters of db2
[LoD, HiD, LoR, HiR] = wfilters('db2');
LoR
[sum(LoR), norm(LoR)]
isequal(LoD, fliplr(LoR))
F = dbwavf('db2');
max(abs(F*sqrt(2) - LoR)) < 1e-15
numel(symwavf('sym4'))
subplot(2, 2, 1), stem(LoD), title('LoD')
subplot(2, 2, 2), stem(HiD), title('HiD')
subplot(2, 2, 3), stem(LoR), title('LoR')
subplot(2, 2, 4), stem(HiR), title('HiR')

@ qmf, orthfilt, biorfilt
Filters made from a scaling filter: the mirror filter, and the four filters of an orthogonal or biorthogonal wavelet.

`qmf` reverses a filter and changes the sign of every other coefficient: the highpass filter that goes with a lowpass one. `orthfilt` makes the four filters of an orthogonal wavelet from its scaling filter (scaled to norm 1). `biorfilt` makes them from a biorthogonal pair, the decomposition and reconstruction scaling filters, each scaled to sum sqrt(2) and padded to one even length.

# Syntax
Y = qmf(X)
    The quadrature mirror filter.
[LoD, HiD, LoR, HiR] = orthfilt(W)
    From an orthogonal scaling filter.
[LoD, HiD, LoR, HiR] = biorfilt(DF, RF)
    From a biorthogonal pair.
# Examples
## db3 from its scaling filter, and the bior2.2 pair
qmf([1 2 3 4])
[LoD, HiD, LoR, HiR] = orthfilt(dbwavf('db3'));
[L2, H2] = wfilters('db3', 'r');
max(abs([LoR - L2, HiR - H2])) < 1e-15
DF = [-1 2 6 2 -1]/8; RF = [1 2 1]/4;
[LoD, HiD, LoR, HiR] = biorfilt(DF, RF);
LoD
LoR
[Lb, Hb, Rb, Gb] = wfilters('bior2.2');
max(abs([LoD - Lb, HiD - Hb, LoR - Rb, HiR - Gb]))

@ dwtmode, wmaxlev
How the discrete transforms extend a signal past its ends, and how deep a decomposition can usefully go.

A filter at the ends of a signal reaches past them, and the extension mode says what it finds there: `'sym'` (the signal mirrored, the default), `'symw'`, `'asym'`, `'asymw'`, `'zpd'` (zeros), `'sp0'`, `'sp1'`, `'ppd'` (periodic) or `'per'` (periodization, which gives exactly half as many coefficients). The mode holds for every discrete transform until changed; each also takes `'mode', m` for one call. `wmaxlev` gives the deepest level at which some coefficient is still free of the extension.

# Syntax
dwtmode(mode)
    Set, and said so.
dwtmode(mode, 'nodisp')
    Set quietly.
m = dwtmode('status')
    The mode in force.
L = wmaxlev(sz, wname)
    The largest useful level.
# Examples
## Half as many coefficients in periodization
x = 1:8;
[cA, cD] = dwt(x, 'db2');
numel(cA)
dwtmode('per', 'nodisp')
[cA, cD] = dwt(x, 'db2');
numel(cA)
dwtmode('sym', 'nodisp')
dwtmode('status')
wmaxlev(1000, 'db4')

@ dwt, idwt
One level of the discrete wavelet transform, and its inverse.

`dwt` filters a signal by the decomposition lowpass and highpass filters and keeps every second sample: the approximation coefficients cA (the slow part) and the detail coefficients cD (the fast part), each about half as long. `idwt` puts the signal back from them: upsampled, filtered by the reconstruction filters and added. Either input may be [] for zeros.

# Syntax
[cA, cD] = dwt(x, wname)
    By a wavelet's name.
[cA, cD] = dwt(x, LoD, HiD)
    By filters.
x = idwt(cA, cD, wname, L)
    The middle L samples of the inverse.
[cA, cD] = dwt(..., 'mode', m)
    With an extension mode for this call.
# Examples
## A ramp by Haar, and back by db2
x = [1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8];
[cA, cD] = dwt(x, 'haar')
[cA2, cD2] = dwt(x, 'db2');
y = idwt(cA2, cD2, 'db2', numel(x));
max(abs(y - x)) < 1e-12

@ wavedec, waverec, appcoef, detcoef
A multilevel decomposition: dwt repeated on the approximations, and the signal back from it.

`wavedec` applies `dwt` n times, each time to the approximation the last one left, and packs the coefficients into one vector c = [cA_n, cD_n, ..., cD_1] with their lengths in l (the signal's length last). `waverec` reverses it exactly. `appcoef` gives the approximation coefficients at a level, `detcoef` the details of one level or several.

# Syntax
[c, l] = wavedec(x, n, wname)
    To level n.
x = waverec(c, l, wname)
    The signal back.
A = appcoef(c, l, wname, n)
    The approximation at level n.
D = detcoef(c, l, n)
    The details of level n.
D = detcoef(c, l, 'cells')
    Every level, level 1 first.
# Examples
## A tone with a step, to level 4
t = linspace(0, 1, 512);
x = sin(2*pi*5*t) + 0.5*(t > 0.6);
[c, l] = wavedec(x, 4, 'db4');
l
xr = waverec(c, l, 'db4');
max(abs(xr - x)) < 1e-10
a4 = appcoef(c, l, 'db4', 4);
D = detcoef(c, l, 'cells');
[numel(a4), cellfun(@numel, D)]

@ wrcoef, upcoef, wenergy
The parts of a signal from one level of its decomposition, and the energy in each.

`wrcoef` reconstructs the signal from one kind of coefficient alone, the others set to zero: `'a'` the approximation at level n, `'d'` the details of level n. The approximation at level n and the details of levels 1 to n add up to the signal. `upcoef` reconstructs a vector of coefficients straight up n levels. `wenergy` gives the percentage of the coefficients' energy in the approximation and in the details of each level.

# Syntax
X = wrcoef('a', c, l, wname, n)
    The approximation at level n.
X = wrcoef('d', c, l, wname, n)
    The details of level n.
Y = upcoef(type, x, wname, n)
    Coefficients reconstructed n levels up.
[Ea, Ed] = wenergy(c, l)
    The shares of the energy.
# Examples
## The parts add up to the signal
t = linspace(0, 1, 512);
x = sin(2*pi*5*t) + 0.5*(t > 0.6);
[c, l] = wavedec(x, 4, 'db4');
A4 = wrcoef('a', c, l, 'db4', 4);
S = A4;
for k = 1:4
    S = S + wrcoef('d', c, l, 'db4', k);
end
max(abs(S - x)) < 1e-10
[Ea, Ed] = wenergy(c, l)
upcoef('a', [1 2], 'haar', 2)
subplot(3, 1, 1), plot(t, x), ylabel('x')
subplot(3, 1, 2), plot(t, A4), ylabel('A_4')
subplot(3, 1, 3), plot(t, wrcoef('d', c, l, 'db4', 1)), ylabel('D_1')

@ dwt2, idwt2
One level of the discrete wavelet transform of an image, and its inverse.

`dwt2` applies `dwt` along the rows and then down the columns: the approximation cA (lowpass both ways), the horizontal details cH (highpass down the columns, so horizontal edges), the vertical details cV and the diagonal ones cD, each about a quarter of the image. `idwt2` puts the image back; any of the four may be [] for zeros.

# Syntax
[cA, cH, cV, cD] = dwt2(X, wname)
    By a wavelet's name.
[cA, cH, cV, cD] = dwt2(X, LoD, HiD)
    By filters.
X = idwt2(cA, cH, cV, cD, wname)
    The image back.
X = idwt2(cA, cH, cV, cD, wname, S)
    Its middle S(1)-by-S(2) part.
# Examples
## A disc and a step, by Haar
[x, y] = meshgrid(1:64);
X = double((x - 32).^2 + (y - 32).^2 < 400) + 0.5*(x > 48);
[cA, cH, cV, cD] = dwt2(X, 'haar');
size(cA)
Y = idwt2(cA, cH, cV, cD, 'haar');
max(abs(Y(:) - X(:))) < 1e-12
subplot(2, 2, 1), imagesc(cA), axis image, title('approximation')
subplot(2, 2, 2), imagesc(cH), axis image, title('horizontal')
subplot(2, 2, 3), imagesc(cV), axis image, title('vertical')
subplot(2, 2, 4), imagesc(cD), axis image, title('diagonal')
colormap gray

@ wavedec2, waverec2, appcoef2, detcoef2, wrcoef2
A multilevel decomposition of an image, and the image back from it or from one part.

`wavedec2` applies `dwt2` n times to the approximations and packs every coefficient into one row C, with the sizes in S (the approximation's first, then each level's details from the deepest, then the image's). `waverec2` reverses it. `appcoef2` gives the approximation at a level, `detcoef2` the horizontal, vertical or diagonal details (`'all'` gives the three), and `wrcoef2` the image reconstructed from one kind of coefficient at a level.

# Syntax
[C, S] = wavedec2(X, n, wname)
    To level n.
X = waverec2(C, S, wname)
    The image back.
A = appcoef2(C, S, wname, n)
    The approximation at level n.
[H, V, D] = detcoef2('all', C, S, n)
    The details of level n.
X = wrcoef2(type, C, S, wname, n)
    One part: 'a', 'h', 'v' or 'd'.
# Examples
## Two levels of db2
[x, y] = meshgrid(1:64);
X = double((x - 32).^2 + (y - 32).^2 < 400) + 0.5*(x > 48);
[C, S] = wavedec2(X, 2, 'db2');
S
Xr = waverec2(C, S, 'db2');
max(abs(Xr(:) - X(:))) < 1e-10
A2 = appcoef2(C, S, 'db2', 2);
[H1, V1, D1] = detcoef2('all', C, S, 1);
[size(A2); size(H1)]
subplot(1, 3, 1), imagesc(X), axis image, title('image')
subplot(1, 3, 2), imagesc(wrcoef2('a', C, S, 'db2', 2)), axis image, title('approximation, level 2')
subplot(1, 3, 3), imagesc(wrcoef2('h', C, S, 'db2', 1)), axis image, title('horizontal, level 1')
colormap gray

@ swt, iswt, swt2, iswt2
The stationary wavelet transform: no samples dropped, so that a shift of the signal only shifts its coefficients.

`swt` filters by the wavelet's filters with 2^(k-1) - 1 zeros between their taps at level k, periodically and without keeping every second sample: each level's approximations and details are as long as the signal (whose length must be a multiple of 2^n). The transform is redundant but shift-invariant, which suits denoising and finding where things happen. `iswt` inverts it; `swt2` and `iswt2` do the same for images, a page for each level.

# Syntax
[swa, swd] = swt(x, n, wname)
    A row for each level.
x = iswt(swa, swd, wname)
    The signal back.
[A, H, V, D] = swt2(X, n, wname)
    Of an image.
X = iswt2(A, H, V, D, wname)
    The image back.
# Examples
## A tone with a pulse, three levels
n = 256;
t = (0:n-1)/n;
x = sin(2*pi*4*t) + (abs(t - 0.5) < 0.05);
[swa, swd] = swt(x, 3, 'db2');
size(swd)
max(abs(iswt(swa, swd, 'db2') - x)) < 1e-10
[~, swds] = swt(circshift(x, 5), 3, 'db2');
max(abs(swds(1, :) - circshift(swd(1, :), 5))) < 1e-12
subplot(4, 1, 1), plot(x), ylabel('x'), axis tight
for k = 1:3
    subplot(4, 1, k + 1), plot(swd(k, :)), ylabel(sprintf('d_%d', k)), axis tight
end
## An image
X = magic(8);
[A, H, V, D] = swt2(X, 2, 'haar');
size(A)
Xr = iswt2(A, H, V, D, 'haar');
max(abs(Xr(:) - X(:))) < 1e-10

@ modwt, imodwt, modwtmra
The maximal overlap discrete wavelet transform, and the multiresolution analysis it gives.

`modwt` gives the wavelet coefficients W_1 to W_J and the scaling coefficients V_J of a signal of any length, each as long as the signal: like the stationary transform, shift-invariant, and its coefficients keep the signal's energy (the sum of their squares is that of the signal). `imodwt` inverts it. `modwtmra` splits the signal into details D_1 to D_J and a smooth S_J, each as long as the signal and lined up with it in time, which add up to the signal. The wavelet is `'sym4'` by default.

# Syntax
w = modwt(x, wname, J)
    J levels.
x = imodwt(w, wname)
    The signal back.
mra = modwtmra(w, wname)
    The details and the smooth, a row each.
w = modwt(..., 'reflection')
    The signal mirrored at its end first.
# Examples
## A slow wave and a burst
n = 500;
t = (0:n-1)/100;
x = sin(2*pi*0.5*t) + 0.5*sin(2*pi*12*t).*(t > 2.5);
w = modwt(x, 'sym4', 4);
size(w)
[sum(w(:).^2), sum(x.^2)]
max(abs(imodwt(w, 'sym4') - x)) < 1e-10
mra = modwtmra(w, 'sym4');
max(abs(sum(mra, 1) - x)) < 1e-10
for k = 1:5
    subplot(5, 1, k), plot(t, mra(k, :)), axis tight
end
subplot(5, 1, 1), title('D1 to D4 and S4')
