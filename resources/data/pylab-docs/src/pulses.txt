@ snr, thd, sinad, sfdr
Measures of a tone's purity: signal to noise, harmonic distortion, signal to noise and distortion, and the spurious-free dynamic range.

Each looks at the spectrum of a signal holding one main tone (through a Kaiser window of beta 38). `snr` gives the tone's power against the noise, with DC and the first n harmonics (6) left out; `thd` the power of harmonics 2 to n against the fundamental's (in dBc, so negative); `sinad` the tone's power against everything else but DC, harmonics included; `sfdr` the tone's power against the largest other component. A tone's power is that of its spectral lobe. `snr(x, y)` is simply 10 log10 of the powers of a signal and its noise.

# Syntax
r = snr(x)
    In dB.
[r, noisepow] = snr(x, fs, n)
    With the noise's power, n harmonics left out.
r = snr(x, y)
    A signal against its noise.
[r, harmpow, harmfreq] = thd(x, fs, n)
    Harmonic distortion, with each harmonic's power and frequency.
[r, totdistpow] = sinad(x, fs)
    Signal to noise and distortion.
[r, spurpow, spurfreq] = sfdr(x, fs)
    The spurious-free dynamic range.
# Examples
## A tone with a second and third harmonic, in noise
fs = 48000;
t = (0:4799)'/fs;
x = sin(2*pi*1000*t) + 0.01*sin(2*pi*2000*t) + 0.003*sin(2*pi*3000*t) + 1e-3*randn(size(t));
round([snr(x, fs), thd(x, fs), sinad(x, fs), sfdr(x, fs)], 1)
[~, hp, hf] = thd(x, fs, 3);
round(hf')
round(hp', 1)
thd(x, fs)

@ statelevels, midcross
A two-state signal's two levels, and where it crosses midway between them.

`statelevels` finds the low and high levels from a histogram of the signal (100 bins): in each half of the range, the average of the samples in its fullest bin. `midcross` gives the times at which each transition crosses the mid-reference level (50% of the way, `'MidPercentReferenceLevel'`), found by linear interpolation; a transition runs from one state's band (`'Tolerance'`, 2% of the levels' difference) to the other's. Times come from fs (the first sample at 0) or the samples' times t. Without outputs, both draw.

# Syntax
levels = statelevels(x)
    The low and high levels.
[levels, histogram, bins] = statelevels(x, nbins)
    With the histogram.
c = midcross(x, fs)
    The mid-reference crossings.
[c, midlev] = midcross(x, t, 'MidPercentReferenceLevel', p, 'StateLevels', lv)
    At another reference, with levels given.
# Examples
## A clock signal with slow edges
fs = 1000;
t = (0:999)'/fs;
x = 1.5 + 1.5*tanh(20*sin(2*pi*5*t)) + 0.02*randn(size(t));
round(statelevels(x), 2)
c = midcross(x, fs);
round(c(1:4)', 3)
midcross(x, fs)

@ risetime, falltime, slewrate
How fast a two-state signal's transitions are.

`risetime` gives the time each rising transition takes from the lower reference level to the upper (10% and 90% of the way between the state levels, `'PercentReferenceLevels'`); `falltime` the same for falling transitions; `slewrate` the difference of the reference levels over each transition's duration, negative when falling. The further outputs give the crossing times and the reference levels. Without outputs, each draws.

# Syntax
r = risetime(x, fs)
    Rise times.
[r, lowercross, uppercross, lowerref, upperref] = risetime(x, t, 'PercentReferenceLevels', [lo hi])
    With the crossings and references.
f = falltime(x, fs)
    Fall times.
s = slewrate(x, fs)
    Slew rates.
# Examples
## A trapezoidal pulse
t = (0:0.01:10)';
x = interp1([0 2 3 6 8 10], [0 0 5 5 0 0], t);
r = risetime(x, t)
f = falltime(x, t)
round(slewrate(x, t)', 2)
risetime(x, t)

@ pulsewidth, pulsesep, pulseperiod, dutycycle
The widths, separations and periods of a two-state signal's pulses, and their duty cycles.

A positive pulse rises then falls (`'Polarity'`, `'negative'` for the opposite). Its width runs between its mid-reference crossings; the separation from its end to the next pulse's start; the period from its start to the next one's; the duty cycle is the width over the period. The further outputs give the crossing times. Without outputs, each draws.

# Syntax
w = pulsewidth(x, fs)
    Widths.
[w, initcross, finalcross, midlev] = pulsewidth(x, t, 'Polarity', p)
    With the crossings.
s = pulsesep(x, fs)
    Separations.
p = pulseperiod(x, fs)
    Periods.
d = dutycycle(x, fs)
    Duty cycles.
# Examples
## A pulse train at 30% duty
fs = 1000;
t = (0:1999)'/fs;
x = double(square(2*pi*4*t, 30) > 0);
x = filter(ones(5, 1)/5, 1, x);
w = pulsewidth(x, fs);
[numel(w), numel(pulsesep(x, fs))]
round([mean(w), mean(pulsesep(x, fs)), mean(pulseperiod(x, fs)), mean(dutycycle(x, fs))], 4)
pulsewidth(x, fs)

@ overshoot, undershoot, settlingtime
How a two-state signal's transitions overshoot, undershoot and settle.

`overshoot` gives, in percent of the levels' difference, the largest excursion beyond the final state after each transition (within `'SeekFactor'` times the transition's duration after it reaches the state's band; `'Region', 'preshoot'` looks before the transition instead); `undershoot` the excursion back towards the other state; `settlingtime` the time from each mid-reference crossing until the signal stays within `'Tolerance'` percent of its final state, looked for over D after the crossing (NaN when it has not settled by then). The further outputs give the levels and the times. Without outputs, each draws.

# Syntax
os = overshoot(x, fs)
    Overshoots, in percent.
[os, oslev, osinst] = overshoot(x, t, 'Region', r, 'SeekFactor', k)
    With the levels and times.
us = undershoot(x, fs)
    Undershoots.
s = settlingtime(x, fs, D)
    Settling times.
[s, slev, sinst, tolerance] = settlingtime(x, t, D, 'Tolerance', tol)
    With the levels and times.
# Examples
## A second-order system's step
sys = tf(1, [1 0.6 1]);
t = (0:0.05:30)';
y = [zeros(20, 1); step(sys, t(1:end - 20))];
os = overshoot(y, t)
round(100*exp(-0.3*pi/sqrt(1 - 0.09)), 2)
s = settlingtime(y, t, 25)
overshoot(y, t)
