@ wavefun, centfrq, scal2frq
A wavelet's scaling function and wavelet, its centre frequency, and the frequencies of its scales.

`wavefun` computes the scaling function phi and the wavelet psi of a discrete wavelet by the cascade algorithm (iter refinements, 8 unless given) on its support; a biorthogonal wavelet gives two pairs, for decomposition and reconstruction. With no outputs it draws them. `centfrq` gives the frequency where the wavelet's spectrum is largest, in cycles per unit of x, and `scal2frq` the frequencies of scales, centfrq/(a*delta) with delta the sampling period.

# Syntax
[phi, psi, x] = wavefun(wname, iter)
    The functions on their support.
[phi1, psi1, phi2, psi2, x] = wavefun(wname, iter)
    A biorthogonal wavelet's two pairs.
f = centfrq(wname)
    The centre frequency.
f = scal2frq(a, wname, delta)
    The frequencies of the scales a.
# Examples
## db4
[phi, psi, x] = wavefun('db4', 8);
[x(1), x(end)]
f = centfrq('db4')
scal2frq([1 2 4 8], 'db4', 0.01)
subplot(1, 2, 1), plot(x, phi), title('scaling function \phi'), grid on
subplot(1, 2, 2), plot(x, psi), title('wavelet \psi'), grid on

@ cwt, icwt, cwtfreqbounds
The continuous wavelet transform: how much of each frequency a signal holds at each moment, and the signal back.

`cwt` correlates the signal with the wavelet stretched to many scales, ten voices to an octave, computed by FFT: a row for each scale, its frequency in f (Hz with a sampling frequency, else cycles per sample), smallest scale (highest frequency) first. The wavelets are analytic, `'morse'` (the default), `'amor'` (analytic Morlet) or `'bump'`, scaled so that a cosine of amplitude A gives abs(wt) = A at its frequency. coi is the cone of influence: below it, at each time, the wavelet reaches past an end of the signal. With no outputs the scalogram is drawn, the cone dashed. `icwt` gives the signal back (closely, not exactly), and `cwtfreqbounds` the frequency range cwt would cover.

# Syntax
[wt, f, coi] = cwt(x, fs)
    With a sampling frequency.
[wt, f, coi] = cwt(x, wname, fs)
    By another wavelet.
[wt, f] = cwt(..., 'VoicesPerOctave', nv, 'FrequencyLimits', [f1 f2])
    More voices, fewer frequencies.
cwt(x, fs)
    The scalogram drawn.
xrec = icwt(wt)
    The signal back.
[minf, maxf] = cwtfreqbounds(N, fs)
    The frequencies for N samples.
# Examples
## A tone that changes its pitch
fs = 200;
t = (0:399)/fs;
x = cos(2*pi*10*t).*(t < 1) + 0.5*cos(2*pi*40*t).*(t >= 1);
[wt, f, coi] = cwt(x, fs);
size(wt)
[~, i] = max(abs(wt(:, 100)));
[f(i), abs(wt(i, 100))]
[~, j] = max(abs(wt(:, 300)));
[f(j), abs(wt(j, 300))]
xr = icwt(wt);
norm(xr - x)/norm(x)
[minf, maxf] = cwtfreqbounds(400, fs)
cwt(x, fs)

@ wthresh, wnoisest, thselect, ddencmp, wden
Wavelet denoising by thresholds: the noise's level, a threshold, and the details shrunk.

Noise spreads over every wavelet coefficient while a signal concentrates in a few large ones, so setting the small details to zero removes noise and keeps the signal. `wthresh` thresholds, soft (shrinking every value toward zero by T) or hard (keeping the values beyond T as they are). `wnoisest` estimates the noise's standard deviation from the details, median(abs(cD))/0.6745, robust to the signal's few large coefficients. `thselect` gives a threshold for noise of unit variance: `'sqtwolog'` (sqrt(2 log n), the universal threshold), `'rigrsure'`, `'heursure'` or `'minimaxi'`. `ddencmp` gives the default values; `wden` does it all at each level of a decomposition.

# Syntax
Y = wthresh(X, sorh, T)
    Soft ('s') or hard ('h').
stdc = wnoisest(c, l, s)
    The noise at the levels s.
thr = thselect(x, tptr)
    A threshold.
[thr, sorh, keepapp] = ddencmp('den', 'wv', x)
    The default values.
xd = wden(x, tptr, sorh, scal, n, wname)
    Denoised: scal 'one', 'sln' or 'mln' for the noise's level.
# Examples
## Soft and hard
wthresh([-3 -1 0.5 2 4], 's', 1.5)
wthresh([-3 -1 0.5 2 4], 'h', 1.5)
## A noisy wave with a step
rng default
n = 1024;
t = (0:n-1)/n;
s = 2*sin(4*pi*t) + 2*(t > 0.3 & t < 0.5);
x = s + 0.4*randn(1, n);
[c, l] = wavedec(x, 5, 'sym4');
sigma = wnoisest(c, l, 1)
[thselect(x/sigma, 'sqtwolog'), sqrt(2*log(n))]
[thr, sorh, keepapp] = ddencmp('den', 'wv', x)
xd = wden(x, 'sqtwolog', 's', 'sln', 5, 'sym4');
[norm(x - s), norm(xd - s)]
plot(t, x, 'Color', [0.7 0.7 0.7]), hold on, plot(t, xd, 'LineWidth', 1.5), plot(t, s, 'k--'), hold off
legend('noisy', 'denoised', 'signal')

@ wdenoise
Wavelet denoising in one call, by one of several rules.

The signal (or each column of a matrix) is decomposed by `'sym4'` to the given level, each level's details are thresholded with the noise estimated from the first level (or each level, `'NoiseEstimate', 'LevelDependent'`), and the approximation is kept. `'DenoisingMethod'` is `'Bayes'` (the default: an empirical Bayes rule that adapts the threshold to how sparse each level is), `'SURE'`, `'UniversalThreshold'`, `'Minimax'`, `'BlockJS'` or `'FDR'`; `'ThresholdRule'` chooses soft or hard thresholding where it applies. With no outputs it draws the signal and the denoised one.

# Syntax
xden = wdenoise(x)
    By the defaults.
xden = wdenoise(x, level, Name, Value)
    To a level, by a method.
[xden, denoisedcfs, origcfs] = wdenoise(...)
    With the coefficients.
wdenoise(x)
    Drawn.
# Examples
## Four rules on one signal
rng default
n = 1024;
t = (0:n-1)/n;
s = 2*sin(4*pi*t) + 2*(t > 0.3 & t < 0.5);
x = s + 0.4*randn(1, n);
xb = wdenoise(x);
xs = wdenoise(x, 5, 'DenoisingMethod', 'SURE');
xu = wdenoise(x, 5, 'DenoisingMethod', 'UniversalThreshold', 'ThresholdRule', 'Hard');
[norm(x - s), norm(xb - s), norm(xs - s), norm(xu - s)]
wdenoise(x)

@ wcodemat
A matrix coded as whole numbers for display as an image.

`wcodemat` scales the absolute values of a matrix (or the values themselves, with absol 0) to whole numbers from 1 to nb (16 unless given), over the whole matrix (`'mat'`), each row (`'row'`) or each column (`'col'`): ready for `image` with a colormap of nb colours. Wavelet coefficients, mostly small with a few large, show their structure this way.

# Syntax
Y = wcodemat(X)

@ cwtfilterbank, wt, scales, centerFrequencies, centerPeriods, timeSpreads
The continuous wavelet transform's filters as an object: made once, used for many signals.

`cwtfilterbank` makes the filters `cwt` would use for signals of SignalLength samples, with the same options (sampling frequency, wavelet, voices per octave, frequency limits). `wt` transforms a signal of that length with them; `freqz` draws their frequency responses and `wavelets` gives them in time. `scales`, `centerFrequencies`, `centerPeriods` and `timeSpreads` give each wavelet's scale, peak frequency, its inverse and its standard deviation in time.

# Syntax
fb = cwtfilterbank('SignalLength', N, 'SamplingFrequency', fs, 'VoicesPerOctave', nv)
    The filter bank.
[cfs, f, coi] = wt(fb, x)
    A signal transformed.
s = scales(fb)
    The scales.
f = centerFrequencies(fb)
    The peak frequencies.
p = centerPeriods(fb)
    Their inverses.
s = timeSpreads(fb)
    The wavelets' widths in time.
# Examples
## One bank, its frequencies, a tone through it
fb = cwtfilterbank('SignalLength', 400, 'SamplingFrequency', 400, 'VoicesPerOctave', 4);
f = centerFrequencies(fb);
[numel(f), f(1), f(end)]
max(abs(centerPeriods(fb) - 1./f)) < 1e-12
size(timeSpreads(fb))
t = (0:399)/400;
[cfs, f2] = wt(fb, cos(2*pi*50*t));
[~, i] = max(abs(cfs(:, 200)));
f2(i)
freqz(fb)

@ dwtfilterbank, scalingfunctions, wavelets, framebounds, dwtpassbands
The discrete wavelet transform's iterated filters as an object.

`dwtfilterbank` makes the equivalent filters of a discrete decomposition: at level j, the lowpass filter cascaded j-1 times and then the highpass one, for signals of SignalLength samples. `wavelets` and `scalingfunctions` give them in time (a row for each level, centred; `wavelets` of a `cwtfilterbank` gives its wavelets the same way), `freqz` draws their responses, `dwtpassbands` gives each level's band of frequencies, and `framebounds` how far the bank is from keeping energy exactly (1 and 1 for an orthogonal wavelet).

# Syntax
fb = dwtfilterbank('Wavelet', wname, 'SignalLength', N, 'Level', L, 'SamplingFrequency', fs)
    The filter bank.
[psi, t] = wavelets(fb)
    The wavelets in time.
[phi, t] = scalingfunctions(fb)
    The scaling functions.
[lower, upper] = framebounds(fb)
    The frame bounds.
bands = dwtpassbands(fb)
    Each level's band, the scaling band last.
# Examples
## db4 to level 3
fb = dwtfilterbank('Wavelet', 'db4', 'SignalLength', 256, 'Level', 3, 'SamplingFrequency', 1000);
bands = dwtpassbands(fb)
[lo, hi] = framebounds(fb)
[psi, t] = wavelets(fb);
[phi, t2] = scalingfunctions(fb);
size(psi)
subplot(2, 1, 1), plot(t, psi'), title('wavelets, levels 1 to 3'), xlim([-0.01 0.06])
subplot(2, 1, 2), plot(t2, phi'), title('scaling functions'), xlim([-0.01 0.06])

@ wsst, iwsst
The wavelet synchrosqueezed transform: the scalogram sharpened onto the instantaneous frequencies.

A continuous wavelet transform smears a tone over neighbouring scales. `wsst` moves each coefficient along frequency to the instantaneous frequency its phase shows (the phase's rate of change in time), so that a tone or a chirp becomes a sharp line; it uses the analytic Morlet (`'amor'`) or `'bump'` wavelet, 32 voices to an octave. With no outputs it draws the transform. `iwsst` gives the signal back, or the part of it within a range of frequencies (a column).

# Syntax
[sst, f] = wsst(x, fs)
    The transform and its frequencies.
[sst, f] = wsst(x, fs, wname, 'VoicesPerOctave', nv)
    Another wavelet, other voices.
wsst(x, fs)
    Drawn.
xrec = iwsst(sst)
    The signal back.
xrec = iwsst(sst, wname, f, freqrange)
    The part in a range.
# Examples
## A chirp from 20 Hz upward
fs = 500;
t = (0:383)/fs;
x = cos(2*pi*(20*t + 100*t.^2));
[sst, f] = wsst(x, fs, 'VoicesPerOctave', 12);
size(sst)
[~, i] = max(abs(sst(:, 192)));
[f(i), 20 + 200*t(192)]
xr = iwsst(sst);
norm(xr - x(:))/norm(x)
wsst(x, fs, 'VoicesPerOctave', 12)

@ wcoherence
Wavelet coherence: where in time and frequency two signals move together.

The coherence is the squared magnitude of the smoothed cross wavelet spectrum over the product of the two smoothed spectra, from 0 to 1 at each time and frequency, by the analytic Morlet wavelet. Smoothing in time (as wide as the scale) and across scales is what lets it be less than 1. wcs is the smoothed cross spectrum, whose angle is the phase between the signals. With no outputs it is drawn with the cone of influence.

# Syntax
[wcoh, wcs, f, coi] = wcoherence(x, y, fs)
    The coherence and the cross spectrum.
wcoherence(x, y, fs, 'VoicesPerOctave', nv, 'NumScalesToSmooth', n)
    Drawn.
# Examples
## A shared 2 Hz burst in two noisy signals
rng default
fs = 20;
t = (0:399)/fs;
common = sin(2*pi*2*t).*(t > 8 & t < 14);
x = common + 0.5*randn(size(t));
y = 0.8*circshift(common, 2) + 0.5*randn(size(t));
[wcoh, wcs, f] = wcoherence(x, y, fs);
size(wcoh)
[~, k] = min(abs(f - 2));
[mean(wcoh(k, t > 9 & t < 13)), mean(wcoh(k, t < 6))]
wcoherence(x, y, fs)

@ ewt
The empirical wavelet transform: filters fitted to the signal's own spectrum.

`ewt` finds the largest peaks of the signal's power spectrum (at most MaxNumPeaks, 5, each at least PeakThresholdPercent, 70, of the largest), puts a boundary between each pair of neighbours (at the geometric mean of their frequencies, or the lowest point between them with `'SegmentMethod', 'localmin'`), and builds a filter for each band with smooth edges that together keep the signal exactly. The columns of mra are the components, adding up to the signal; mfb holds the filters' responses and info the peaks and boundaries (in cycles per sample).

# Syntax
[mra, mfb] = ewt(x)
    The components and the filters.
[mra, mfb, info] = ewt(x, 'MaxNumPeaks', n, 'PeakThresholdPercent', p)
    With the peaks and boundaries.
# Examples
## Three tones apart
fs = 1000;
t = (0:999)/fs;
x = cos(2*pi*30*t) + 0.8*cos(2*pi*120*t) + 0.6*cos(2*pi*300*t);
[mra, mfb, info] = ewt(x, 'PeakThresholdPercent', 30);
size(mra)
max(abs(sum(mra, 2) - x(:))) < 1e-10
info.PeakFrequencies'*fs
info.BoundaryFrequencies'*fs
for k = 1:3
    subplot(3, 1, k), plot(t, mra(:, k)), axis tight
end
