@ line
A line added to the current axes, without clearing them.

Unlike `plot`, `line` is a low-level function: it adds to the axes as they are, whatever hold says, and takes no line spec, only names and values. It gives the line object when asked.

# Syntax
line(x, y)
    A line in 2-D.
line(x, y, z)
    In 3-D.
h = line(..., 'Name', value)
    With properties.
# Examples
## Lines added to a plot
x = linspace(0, 2*pi, 100);
plot(x, sin(x))
line(x, cos(x), 'Color', 'r', 'LineStyle', '--')
h = line([0 2*pi], [0 0], 'Color', [0.5 0.5 0.5]);
numel(get(gca, 'Children'))
# See also
plot xline

@ ezplot, ezplot3, ezpolar
Functions plotted from text or handles, the older way.

`ezplot` plots f(x) over an interval (-2 pi to 2 pi unless given), or an implicit curve f(x, y) = 0; `ezplot3` a curve x(t), y(t), z(t) in 3-D (t from 0 to 2 pi), marking its end with `'animate'`; `ezpolar` a curve r = f(theta) in polar axes. The functions are text such as `'cos(t)'` or function handles. `fplot`, `fimplicit`, `fplot3` and `fpolarplot` are the newer forms.

# Syntax
ezplot(f, [xmin xmax])
    A function of x.
ezplot3(x, y, z, [tmin tmax])
    A curve in 3-D.
ezpolar(f, [a b])
    In polar axes.
# Examples
## Three older plots
subplot(1, 3, 1), ezplot('x^2 - 2', [-2 2])
subplot(1, 3, 2), ezplot3('cos(t)', 'sin(t)', 't/5', [0 4*pi])
subplot(1, 3, 3), ezpolar('1 + cos(t)')
# See also
fplot fpolarplot

@ scatter3, swarmchart, swarmchart3
Markers in 3-D, and scatter plots with points of the same x spread sideways.

`scatter3` places markers at points in 3-D, with sizes and colours of their own. `swarmchart` spreads the points of each x sideways so that they do not hide each other: by density (wider where they are many, the default), at random (`'rand'`), or not at all; `swarmchart3` spreads in x and y.

# Syntax
scatter3(x, y, z, sz, c)
    Markers in 3-D.
s = swarmchart(x, y, sz, c, 'filled', 'XJitter', 'density', 'XJitterWidth', w)
    A swarm chart.
s = swarmchart3(x, y, z, sz, c)
    In 3-D.
# Examples
## Three groups as a swarm, and a cloud in 3-D
g = [ones(60, 1); 2*ones(60, 1); 3*ones(60, 1)];
y = [randn(60, 1); randn(60, 1) + 1; 0.5*randn(60, 1) - 1];
subplot(1, 2, 1), swarmchart(g, y, 12, 'filled'), xticks(1:3)
t = linspace(0, 4*pi, 150)';
subplot(1, 2, 2), scatter3(cos(t), sin(t), t, 15, t, 'filled'), view(-30, 25)
# See also
scatter boxchart

@ bubblechart, bubblechart3, polarbubblechart, bubblesize, bubblelim
Bubbles at points, their areas growing with a size; in 2-D, 3-D and polar axes.

The smallest size in the axes gets the smallest bubble and the largest the largest: `bubblesize` sets their diameters in points (1 and 20 to begin with), `bubblelim` the sizes they stand for (the data's range unless set). Colours are one for all, a row for each bubble, or values through the colormap; the faces are partly see-through, so that overlapping bubbles show.

# Syntax
bubblechart(x, y, sz, c)
    Bubbles.
bubblechart3(x, y, z, sz, c)
    In 3-D.
polarbubblechart(theta, rho, sz, c)
    In polar axes.
bubblesize([dmin dmax])
    The bubbles' diameters.
bubblelim([lo hi])
    The sizes they stand for.
# Examples
## Cities as bubbles
lon = [18.07 11.97 13.00 17.64 20.26];
lat = [59.33 57.71 55.61 59.86 63.83];
pop = [984 590 362 242 130];
bubblechart(lon, lat, pop, pop)
bubblesize([5 30])
xlabel('longitude'), ylabel('latitude')
bubblelim
# See also
scatter geobubble

@ barh, bar3, bar3h, pareto
Bars sideways and in 3-D, and bars in order with their running percentage.

`barh` draws horizontal bars; `bar3` and `bar3h` bars as blocks in 3-D (a matrix's columns side by side, or `'grouped'` and `'stacked'`). `pareto` sorts bars from the largest down and draws their cumulative percentage over them, on a second axis: which few causes make up most of the total.

# Syntax
barh(x, y)
    Horizontal bars.
bar3(y, Z, width)
    Bars in 3-D.
bar3h(Z)
    Along the x-axis.
[H, ax] = pareto(y, names, threshold)
    Sorted, with the running percentage.
# Examples
## Faults by cause
counts = [12 45 7 30 6];
names = {'seal', 'valve', 'wiring', 'pump', 'other'};
subplot(1, 2, 1), barh(counts), yticklabels(names)
subplot(1, 2, 2), pareto(counts, names)
## Blocks in 3-D
bar3(magic(4))
# See also
bar histogram

@ hist, histogram2, rose
Histograms the older way, of two variables, and of angles.

`hist` draws a histogram of y in nbins bins (10), or with outputs gives the counts and the bin centres without drawing. `histogram2` bins pairs of values on a grid and draws the counts as bars in 3-D. `rose` is an angle histogram in polar axes (`polarhistogram` is the newer form).

# Syntax
[counts, centers] = hist(y, nbins)
    Counts and centres.
hist(y, nbins)
    Drawn.
histogram2(X, Y, nbins)
    Of pairs.
rose(theta, nbins)
    Of angles.
# Examples
## Counts, a joint histogram and directions
y = randn(500, 1);
[n, c] = hist(y, 5);
[n; round(c, 2)]
x2 = randn(2000, 1);
y2 = 0.6*x2 + 0.8*randn(2000, 1);
subplot(1, 2, 1), histogram2(x2, y2, [12 12])
subplot(1, 2, 2), rose(pi/4 + 0.4*randn(300, 1), 24)
# See also
histogram histcounts polarhistogram

@ fill, fill3, patch
Filled polygons.

`fill` and `fill3` draw polygons from the columns of x, y (and z), each filled with a colour (or interpolated from colours at its vertices), clearing the axes unless held. `patch` adds a polygon object to the axes as they are, from points or from faces and vertices (as `isosurface` gives), with properties such as `'FaceColor'`, `'FaceAlpha'` and `'EdgeColor'`.

# Syntax
fill(x, y, c)
    Polygons.
fill3(x, y, z, c)
    In 3-D.
patch(x, y, c)
    A polygon object.
patch('Faces', F, 'Vertices', V, 'FaceColor', c)
    From faces and vertices.
# Examples
## A hexagon, a coloured triangle, a see-through square
t = (0:5)*pi/3;
fill(cos(t), sin(t), [0.3 0.6 0.9]), axis equal, hold on
patch([1.5 3 2.2], [-1 -0.5 1], [1 2 3])
patch('Faces', [1 2 3 4], 'Vertices', [0 -0.5; 2 -0.5; 2 0.5; 0 0.5], 'FaceColor', 'r', 'FaceAlpha', 0.3)
hold off
# See also
area polyshape

@ polarscatter, polaraxes, polar
Markers in polar coordinates, polar axes, and the older polar plot.

`polarscatter` places markers at angles theta (radians) and radii rho, with sizes and colours of their own. `polaraxes` makes polar axes (angles round a circle, radii out from the middle), alone or in a tile of `subplot`, with properties such as `ThetaZeroLocation`, `ThetaDir` and `RLim`. `polar` is the older form of `polarplot`.

# Syntax
polarscatter(theta, rho, sz, c, 'filled')
    Markers.
pax = polaraxes('Name', value, ...)
    Polar axes.
polar(theta, rho)
    The older plot.
# Examples
## Wind directions and speeds, north at the top, clockwise
dir = deg2rad([10 40 45 80 200 220 230 250 260 300]);
speed = [3 5 4 6 8 9 7 10 6 4];
pax = polaraxes('ThetaZeroLocation', 'top', 'ThetaDir', 'clockwise');
polarscatter(pax, dir, speed, 40, speed, 'filled')
pax.RLim = [0 12];
# See also
polarplot polarhistogram

@ quiver3, compass, feather
Arrows in 3-D, from the origin, and along an axis.

`quiver3` draws an arrow from each point (x, y, z) along (u, v, w), scaled so that they do not overlap unless the scale is 0 or `'off'`. `compass` draws arrows from the origin in polar axes, one for each pair (U, V) or complex number; `feather` draws them from evenly spaced points along the x-axis, a time series of directions.

# Syntax
quiver3(x, y, z, u, v, w, scale)
    Arrows in 3-D.
compass(U, V)
    From the origin.
compass(Z)
    Of complex numbers.
feather(U, V)
    Along the x-axis.
# Examples
## Normals of a surface, and a day's wind
[x, y] = meshgrid(-1:0.5:1);
z = x.^2 - y.^2;
[nx, ny, nz] = surfnorm(x, y, z);
subplot(1, 3, 1), surf(x, y, z), hold on, quiver3(x, y, z, nx, ny, nz, 0.5), hold off
w = 3*exp(1i*linspace(0, pi, 8));
subplot(1, 3, 2), compass(w)
subplot(1, 3, 3), feather(real(w), imag(w))
# See also
quiver surfnorm

@ stem3, ribbon, pie3
Stems in 3-D, columns as ribbons, and a pie in 3-D.

`stem3` draws stems from z = 0 up to each value, with markers at their ends; `ribbon` draws each column of Y as a ribbon of a width (0.75); `pie3` draws a pie chart as a solid.

# Syntax
stem3(X, Y, Z, 'filled')
    Stems in 3-D.
ribbon(X, Y, width)
    Ribbons.
pie3(x, explode, labels)
    A solid pie.
# Examples
## Three 3-D charts
[X, Y] = meshgrid(1:5);
subplot(1, 3, 1), stem3(X, Y, exp(-((X - 3).^2 + (Y - 3).^2)/4), 'filled')
subplot(1, 3, 2), ribbon(peaks(20))
subplot(1, 3, 3), pie3([3 2 1], [0 1 0])

@ animatedline, addpoints, clearpoints, getpoints
A line that points are added to as a program runs.

An animated line starts empty (or with points); `addpoints` adds points, drawn at the next `drawnow`, the oldest dropped beyond `'MaximumNumPoints'`; `clearpoints` takes them all off; `getpoints` gives them. It takes a line's properties, so that colour, width and markers can be set.

# Syntax
an = animatedline('Color', c, 'MaximumNumPoints', n)
    An animated line.
addpoints(an, x, y)
    Points added.
clearpoints(an)
    Points taken off.
[x, y] = getpoints(an)
    Its points.
# Examples
## A trace that keeps its last 50 points
an = animatedline('Color', 'b', 'MaximumNumPoints', 50);
axis([0 10 -1.2 1.2])
for t = linspace(0, 10, 200)
    addpoints(an, t, sin(2*t));
    drawnow limitrate
end
[x, y] = getpoints(an);
[numel(x), round(x(1), 2), x(end)]
# See also
drawnow plot

@ xline, yline, xregion, yregion
Lines and shaded bands across the whole axes.

`xline` draws vertical lines at x values and `yline` horizontal ones, reaching across the axes whatever the limits become and not changing them, with a line spec and labels. `xregion` and `yregion` shade bands between pairs of values. Each gives its objects, whose `Value` moves them.

# Syntax
xl = xline(x, LineSpec, label)
    Vertical lines.
yl = yline(y, LineSpec, label)
    Horizontal lines.
xr = xregion(x1, x2, 'FaceColor', c)
    A vertical band.
yr = yregion(y1, y2)
    A horizontal band.
# Examples
## Limits and an interval marked
t = linspace(0, 10, 300);
y = sin(t) + 0.1*t;
plot(t, y), hold on
yline([-0.5 1.5], '--', {'low', 'high'});
xline(5, 'r', 'event');
xregion(2, 3.5);
yregion(0.9, 1.1, 'FaceColor', 'g');
hold off

@ plotyy
Two plots with a y axis each, the older way.

The first plot uses the left axis and the second the right, both sharing x; each may be drawn by its own function (`'plot'`, `'semilogy'` and the like). The outputs are both axes and both plots. `yyaxis` does the same in steps.

# Syntax
[ax, h1, h2] = plotyy(x1, y1, x2, y2)
    Two plots.
[ax, h1, h2] = plotyy(x1, y1, x2, y2, fun1, fun2)
    By functions of their own.
# Examples
## A temperature and a pressure
t = 0:0.5:12;
[ax, h1, h2] = plotyy(t, 20 + 5*sin(t/2), t, 1000*exp(-t/8), 'plot', 'semilogy');
ylabel(ax(1), 'temperature'), ylabel(ax(2), 'pressure'), xlabel('hours')
numel(ax)
# See also
yyaxis

@ gplot, treelayout, treeplot, etreeplot
Graphs and trees drawn from matrices and parent lists.

`gplot` draws an edge for each nonzero A(i, j) between the points in rows i and j of xy (or gives the lines' coordinates). `treelayout` gives positions for drawing a tree from its parent list (parent(k) is node k's parent, 0 for a root): leaves evenly across, each parent above its children. `treeplot` draws the tree; `etreeplot` the elimination tree of a sparse matrix.

# Syntax
gplot(A, xy, LineSpec)
    A graph.
[x, y, h, s] = treelayout(parent)
    A tree's layout.
treeplot(parent, nodeSpec, edgeSpec)
    A tree.
etreeplot(A)
    An elimination tree.
# Examples
## A ring of six, and a small family tree
k = (0:5)';
xy = [cos(2*pi*k/6), sin(2*pi*k/6)];
A = diag(ones(5, 1), 1) + diag(1, -5);
subplot(1, 2, 1), gplot(A + A', xy, 'o-'), axis equal
parent = [0 1 1 2 2 3 3 3];
[x, y, h] = treelayout(parent);
h
subplot(1, 2, 2), treeplot(parent)

graph plot etree
